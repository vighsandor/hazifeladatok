❓ Query: "What are the XAdES baseline signature levels?"

(a) HyDE Generated Answer:
"The XAdES (XML Advanced Electronic Signatures) standard defines several baseline signature levels to ensure the integrity and authenticity of electronic documents. These levels include XAdES-BES (Basic Electronic Signature), which provides the essential signature functionalities; XAdES-EPES (Explicit Policy Electronic Signature), which incorporates a signature policy; XAdES-T (Timestamped Electronic Signature), which adds a trusted timestamp; and XAdES-C (Complete Electronic Signature), which includes all relevant signature data and validation information. Additionally, XAdES-XL (Extended Long-term Electronic Signature) is designed for long-term validation, ensuring that the signature remains verifiable over time. Each level builds upon the previous one, enhancing the security and trustworthiness of the electronic signature."

❓ Query: "Which data must a qualified certificate for a natural person contain?"

(a) HyDE Generated Answer:
"A qualified certificate for a natural person, as defined by ETSI standards, must contain the following essential data: the identity of the certificate holder, including the individual's name and, where applicable, additional identifiers such as a unique identification number; the public key of the certificate holder; the identity of the issuing certification authority; the validity period of the certificate; and a unique serial number. Additionally, the certificate must include a statement indicating that it is a qualified certificate, as well as the relevant policies under which it was issued. This information ensures the integrity and authenticity of the electronic signature associated with the certificate."

❓ Query: "How is a PAdES document time-stamp signature defined?"

(a) HyDE Generated Answer:
"A PAdES (PDF Advanced Electronic Signature) document time-stamp signature is defined as a cryptographic mechanism that provides a reliable proof of the existence of a signed document at a specific point in time. This is achieved by applying a time-stamp token, which is generated by a trusted time-stamping authority (TSA), to the signed document. The time-stamp token includes the hash of the signed content along with the TSA's digital signature, ensuring the integrity and authenticity of the time-stamp. In accordance with ETSI TS 102 778, the time-stamp must be compliant with the relevant standards to ensure interoperability and long-term validation of the electronic signature."
